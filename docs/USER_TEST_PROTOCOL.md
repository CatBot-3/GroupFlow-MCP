# Small formative test: coordinating a changed evening

Status: protocol prepared; **no independent participant results collected**. This is a product-learning exercise, not evidence of market size.

Recruit three to five people who actually organize activities with friends or family. Use synthetic names, places and conditions; do not ask for a participant's real calendar or private reasons. Tell them that Alexa+, the venue event and bookings are simulated, and obtain consent before recording anything.

Give each participant the same three tasks without explaining the implementation:

1. Find out what has changed about the agreed Friday evening and identify the proposed replacement.
2. Decide which parts still have valid confirmations and what they personally need to review; give their own answer.
3. Explain whether the plan is actually agreed, who can commit it, and why another person's claimed approval does not count.

For the optional technical-comprehension task, ask: “What did the assistant read on its last turn? Can it read another person's private limit?” Let them use the memory map and trace, then ask them to distinguish the current snapshot from the earlier model input.

Record task completion, elapsed time, wrong turns, help requested, misunderstood labels, whether they understood the confirmation scope, and one brief explanation in their own words. Log failures as well as successes. Use a manual observation sheet or explicit consented recording; tool latency is already available from turn records, and server receipt timestamps establish when an answer was recorded. Do not mistake these timestamps for a complete measure of a person's effort.

For a comparison, counterbalance which interface people see first, keep the same plan and change, and distinguish a full-reconfirmation policy from scoped reuse. Avoid leading them with the “75% fewer requests” result before observing their behavior. Report individual observations and sample size; do not extrapolate three to five participants into population-level accuracy or time-saving claims.

No external invitations, messages or personal-data collection are performed automatically by this repository.
